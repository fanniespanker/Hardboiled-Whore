
# CNML Specification v0.5.1 (Draft)

## Foundations

CNML (Contextual Narrative Markup Language) is a constrained XML vocabulary with semantic rules.

All CNML documents MUST be well‑formed XML.

CNML is primarily an experiential and discourse-oriented representation system.

Experiential content refers to represented subjective or interpretive states, not objective truth claims.

CNML encodes semantic, experiential, structural, temporal, prosodic, and spatial relationships, not canonical presentation.

Evaluation, rendering, playback, and projection are interpreter-dependent realizations of CNML source structure.

Operational interpretation MAY derive additional structure for rendering or execution purposes, but such derived structure MUST NOT be treated as intrinsic source semantics.

---

## Core Evaluation Model

CNML evaluation operates across four primary expressive planes:

```text
Narrative × Temporal × Prosodic × Spatial
```

These planes are independently representable and compositional.

## Planes

| Plane     | Meaning                          |
|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Narrative | discourse, cognition, experience |
| Temporal  | musical/performance progression  |
| Prosodic  | affective modulation             |
| Spatial   | typographic/spatial arrangement  |

---

## Narrative Ontology

`<narrative>` does not represent objective reality.

Narrative content is interpreted as a projection generated by a narrator model.

Narrators MAY be:
- unreliable
- dishonest
- hallucinating
- delusional
- temporally inconsistent
- partially reconstructive

CNML discourse is therefore epistemically unstable by default.

---

## GEL Relationship

CNML MAY contain internally persistent ontologically-oriented metadata and semantic structures.

GEL MAY additionally provide external persistent semantic grounding.

Evaluators MAY:
- privilege internal CNML semantics
- privilege external GEL semantics
- merge both systems
- resolve conflicts heuristically

CNML persistence and GEL persistence are therefore orthogonal but interoperable.

## Prosody

Prosody is a compositional modulation system.

### Dimensions

```xml

<gentle>    <forceful>
<smooth>    <harsh>
<engaged>   <detached>

```

#### Semantics

Prosody:
- modifies expression quality
- does not alter discourse structure
- composes recursively
- is evaluator-interpreted

---

### Integrity Operators

Integrity operators instruct the evaluator to decorate or alter their text to appear degraded according to aesthetic rules determined by the renderer's configuration. These indicate *diegetic* corruption of representation, whether intentional or unintentional.

These are:

```xml
<redacted>
<censored>
<corrupted>
```

#### Semantics

The `mode` attribute is evaluator-defined and MAY correspond to recognizable corruption aesthetics or encoding artifacts. The `severity` attribute is evaluator defined and MAY correspond to corruption that is more or less intense.

Examples:

`<redacted severity="0.8" source="narrator">Black bars and footnotes abound.</redacted>`
…renders as something like `█████ₐ₄₋₃₁ ████ₙ₁₁₋₂ and █████████ₙ₇₋₁₃ ███████ᵥ₅₋₁.

`<censored severity="0.4" source="government">You wouldn't download an Internet.</censored>`
…renders as something like `You wouldn't [CENSORED] an [CENSORED].`

`<corrupted severity="1.0" mode="mojibake">UTF-8 tastes great.</corrupted>`
…renders as something like: `ॕൔᡆᔭጸ ٴѡࡳѴݥᡳᄠg౲ὥᝡ୴ᐮ`

`<corrupted severity="0.3" mode="zalgo">All hail Zalgo!</corrupted>`
…renders as something like `Ä̙̹͆ll̪̺̝ hail̗̲̍ͧ ͅZaḷ̮͓̈́̆͆g͔͚̳̈͑ͫȍ̯̘̍!͐͗`

## CiNnaMoN (Cognitive-Narrative Musical Notation DSL)

CiNnaMoN is a structured, human-readable temporal annotation layer for expressive or performative text segments.

Music elements may appear as semantic content inside ANY CNML container.


### CiNnaMoN Semantic Duality Principle

CiNnaMoN defines duration, beat, and measure-length attributes under a **dual semantic regime**:

---

#### 1. Authorial (Normative) Semantics

All temporal attributes (`time`, `beat`, `duration`) primarily serve a **normative compositional function**:

- to guide authors in producing musically coherent notation
- to constrain structural plausibility of rhythmic expression
- to establish local consistency of temporal intent

In this mode:
- values are treated as **intent descriptors**
- authors are NOT expected to perform arithmetic reasoning
- symbolic forms (including fractions) are expressive, not computational

---

#### 2. Operational (Renderer) Semantics

Temporal attributes are secondarily interpreted by renderers as **operational constraints**:

- to validate structural consistency of the score
- to resolve timing relationships for playback, layout, or simulation
- to detect under- or over-specified temporal structures

In this mode:
- values MAY be normalized or evaluated
- interpretation is implementation-defined
- results MUST NOT be re-encoded back into author-facing syntax

Renderers MAY reject temporal systems that cannot be operationally resolved within their target notation or playback model.

---

#### Non-Reification Constraint

Operational interpretation MUST NOT be treated as part of CiNnaMoN source semantics.

That is:

- computed timing is **derivative**, not intrinsic
- renderer resolution does not modify authorial meaning
- CiNnaMoN text remains invariant under evaluation

---

#### Structural Validation Hierarchy

Renderers MAY apply validation at two levels:

1. **Structural plausibility checks**
   - consistency of measure grouping
   - completeness of required fields
   - continuity constraints for tied or sustained events

2. **Temporal resolution**
   - mapping durations into internal time units
   - scheduling of events for output systems

---

#### Key Principle

CiNnaMoN source notation expresses **temporal intent**, not explicit computation.

Authors describe:
- rhythmic structure
- temporal continuity
- expressive grouping
- performative timing relationships

Renderers MAY:
- validate structural consistency
- resolve durations operationally
- map symbolic durations into playback or layout systems

However:

- operational resolution is derivative
- computed timing is not authorial syntax
- renderer interpretation MUST NOT alter source semantics

CiNnaMoN therefore functions simultaneously as:

- a symbolic notation system for human authors
- a temporal constraint system for evaluators and renderers

without collapsing either role into the other.

---

### CiNnaMoN Grammar

The content model is line-oriented and token-based.

#### Core Element

```xml
<music time="" beat="" key="" mode="" tempo="" mood="">
```

##### Attributes
* Together, `time` and `beat` define the enclosing measure duration for operational interpretation purposes:
  * `time`: the number of beats in a measure (numerator of time signature). Example values: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.5, 3.1415926535, pi, √2.
  * `beat`: the note-value unit representing the beat (inverse duration of the beat). Defines the denominator of the time signature. Example values: 2 = half note, 4 = quarter note, 5 = fifth note, 8 = eighth note, 16 = sixteenth note.
  * Renderers MAY approximate irrational or symbolic temporal values for time and beat.
* `key`: tonal center (A–G with optional accidentals). Examples: `C` for the key of C natural, `Gsharp` for G sharp, `Aflat` for A flat.
* `mode`: modal context (major, minor, lydian, phrygian, dorian, etc.)
* `tempo`: may be descriptive (largo, adagio, moderato, allegretto, etc.) or numeric (real number) in beats per minute (120)
* `mood`: descriptive or desired emotional outcome

---

#### Top-level structure

```
<music> ::= "<music" attributes ">" content "</music>"

attributes ::= (time | beat | key | mode | tempo | mood)*
```

#### Content model

```
content ::= bar ("|" bar)*

bar ::= sequence

sequence ::= element (whitespace element)*
```

#### Element types

```
element ::= note | rest | tie | caesura | speech
```

#### Duration

```
duration ::= ([1-9][0-9]*) "/" ([1-9][0-9]*)   // e.g. 1/4, 3/16, 99/167
```

Examples:
- `7/2`
- `2/3`
- `7/5`
- `3/11`

Durations MAY span multiple measures when interpreted by a renderer as exceeding the enclosing measure duration.

Examples:

```
D:1/4{Two} C:1/4{beats.}

X:2/1{Here are two measures of speech.}

C:7/2{Here are three and a half measures of freely sung vocals.}

R:9/5
```

* Renderers MAY approximate irrational or symbolic temporal values for duration.

---

#### Note syntax

```
note ::= pitch ":" duration "{" lyric "}" 
       | pitch ":" duration
       | pitch "{" lyric "}"
       | pitch

pitch ::= [A-G] accidental? octave?
accidental ::= "#" | "b"
octave ::= -? ( [0] | [1-9][0-9]* )
```

#### Vocalization Semantics

CiNnaMoN note events represent performative pitched articulations.

Lyrics are optional semantic attachments to note events rather than prerequisites for note existence.

An empty lyric body:

```text
{}
```

represents intentionally non-lexical vocalization or unspecified articulation.

Examples include:
- humming
- whistling
- vowel-based singing
- nonverbal melodic performance

Renderers MAY additionally interpret lyricless note events as instrumental or synthesized realizations.

#### Rest syntax

```
rest ::= "R" (":" duration)?
```

#### Tie / sustain syntax

```
tie ::= "~"
```

* Ties connect preceding and following notes or sustain state across elements.

#### Caesura / breath mark syntax

```
caesura ::= "//"
```

* `//` is a **caesura or breath mark**, not a comment.
* CNML does NOT define comments in CiNnaMoN.
* No comment syntax exists.


#### Speech syntax

Speech is a timed vocal articulation primitive. It does not encode pitch; pitch is resolved at evaluation time via renderer configuration, inference, or default policy.

```
speech ::= "X" (":" duration)? "{" lyric "}"
```

* NOTE: Common renderer profiles MAY include syllabic, rhythmic-spoken, or other amelodic vocal realizations.

---

##### Composer-interpreted speech rhythm (structured timing)

Speech MAY be subdivided into multiple articulated (`X`) events for rhythmic articulation.

Example:

```
X:3/8{Here} X:1/8{is} X:1/8{a} X:1/8{phrase.} |
```

Semantics:
- each `X` is a discrete articulation unit
- rhythm is explicitly composed
- timing is fully specified by durations

---

##### Performer-interpreted rhythm (free speech)

Speech MAY be encoded as a single event with internal timing left to interpretation.

Example:

```
X:1/1{Here is a measure of speech.}
```

Semantics:
- `X` defines total temporal window
- internal pacing is performer-determined
- no internal segmentation is specified

---

#### Inheritance Rules (Measure Scope)

Within a given measure (bar):

* The first explicit note MUST define:

  * pitch
  * octave
  * duration
  
* The first explicit rest or explicit speech MUST define:
  
  * duration

* Subsequent `note`s, `rests`, and `speech`es within the same measure MAY omit any of these fields.

  * Missing pitch, octave, or duration values inherit from the most recent explicitly defined values in that measure scope.

* Inheritance resets at each new measure boundary (`|`).

# PÂTE (Positional Affine Text Encoding) — v0.4.x Clean Graph Spec (Finalized)

## 1. Overview

PÂTE is a deterministic **graph-based spatial constraint language** for CNML.

It defines layout as a set of **directed affine constraints between span anchors**, compiled into a static graph and resolved deterministically by the rendering system.

PÂTE is strictly declarative:
- no solver heuristics
- no energy or convergence models
- no recursive geometry dependencies
- no conflict resolution or optimization layer

A PÂTE program is valid only if its constraint graph is well-formed.

---

## 2. System Architecture (Cleaned)

The full CNML spatial pipeline is:

```
CNML → PÂTE → Renderer
```

### 2.1 CNML (Structural Layer)
Defines narrative, semantic, and document structure.

### 2.2 PÂTE (Constraint Generation Layer)
Compiles span syntax into a **directed affine constraint graph**.

### 2.3 Renderer (Evaluation Layer)
Implements deterministic graph resolution:
- computes final anchor positions
- applies affine constraints
- produces final layout (SVG / HTML / PDF / etc.)

> There is no intermediate “Poem DSL engine” layer.

---

## 3. Core Model

PÂTE defines a graph:

```
G = (V, E)
```

Where:

- **V** = (Span, Anchor)
- **E** = affine constraints between anchors

Each edge encodes:

```
⟨A, a⟩ → ⟨B, b⟩ + δ
```

---

## 4. Spans

A span is an immutable, renderer-defined geometric unit.

Each span exposes exactly three implicit anchors:

| Anchor | Meaning |
|--------|--------|
| `<`    | leading edge |
| `|`    | center |
| `>`    | trailing edge |

Anchors are intrinsic projections of span geometry.

---

## 5. Syntax

```ebnf
SPAN :=
  "{{"
    SOURCE_SPAN_ID?
    SOURCE_ALIGN_OP
    TARGET_ALIGN_OP
    TARGET_SPAN_ID
    OFFSET?
    ("::" CONTENT)?
  "}}"
```

Whitespace is semantically inert.

---

### 5.1 Identifier Grammar

```ebnf
SPAN_ID := ID

ID := ID_START ID_CONTINUE*

ID_START :=
  Unicode_Letter

ID_CONTINUE :=
    Unicode_Letter
  | Unicode_Number
  | SYMBOL_ATOM

SYMBOL_ATOM :=
    Unicode_Symbol Variation_Selector?
```

Identifiers are Unicode-aware and permit characters from arbitrary writing systems, including:

- Latin
- Greek
- Cyrillic
- Arabic
- Hebrew
- CJK ideographs
- Hiragana
- Katakana
- Hangul
- historical scripts supported by Unicode

Combining marks MAY appear only after a leading Unicode letter.

Identifiers are normalization-sensitive.

Evaluators are NOT required to canonicalize Unicode normalization forms.

Authors are responsible for maintaining identifier consistency across composed and decomposed Unicode representations.

Characters outside the permitted Unicode categories are invalid in identifiers.

This excludes:
- punctuation
- whitespace
- symbols
- format controls
- zero-width joiners
- emoji

---

#### Identifier Semantics

Identifiers are treated as raw Unicode codepoint sequences.

##### 1. Identity Rule

Two identifiers are equal if and only if their Unicode codepoint sequences are identical.

No normalization or equivalence transformations are applied.

This includes, but is not limited to:
- NFC / NFD normalization
- compatibility decomposition
- grapheme cluster equivalence
- emoji sequence interpretation
- variation selector folding

##### 2. Implementation Requirement

Implementations MUST:

- preserve identifier codepoints exactly as written
- perform equality comparisons using raw sequence comparison
- treat identifiers as opaque strings with no semantic interpretation

##### 3. Validity Scope

The specification defines identifier equality only.

It does not define:
- visual equivalence
- rendering behavior
- user interface normalization
- aliasing or suggestion systems

These behaviors are implementation-defined and outside the scope of PÂTE semantics.

##### 4. Author Responsibility

Identifiers are not validated for visual ambiguity.

Authors are responsible for ensuring that identifiers are:
- unique within their intended scope
- readable enough for their own use cases
- consistent across their own tooling pipelines

The system does not enforce or assist with preventing accidental duplication due to visually similar Unicode sequences.

##### 5. Failure Modes

The following are valid but potentially problematic:

- visually identical identifiers with different codepoints
- semantically unrelated identifiers with similar appearance
- identifiers containing combining marks or variation selectors that affect identity

These conditions are not errors in the specification.

Because identifiers are treated as raw Unicode sequences, small variations in codepoints (including combining marks, variation selectors, and visually similar characters) will produce distinct identifiers.

Authors SHOULD take care when constructing identifiers.

---

## 6. Alignment Model (Cartesian Product)

Let:

```text
ALIGN_OP = { <, |, > }
```

Alignment constraints are elements of:

```text
ALIGN_OP × ALIGN_OP
```

Each alignment expression defines:

```text
(source_align_op, target_align_op)
```

The left alignment operator applies only to the source span.

The right alignment operator applies only to the target span.

Alignment operators are unary projection operators over span geometry.

Each operator selects one of the span’s implicit anchors:

| Operator | Selected anchor |
|----------|-----------------|
| `<` | leading anchor |
| `|` | center anchor |
| `>` | trailing anchor |

The operators themselves are not anchors and do not form composite tokens.

Meaning:

```text
A[source_align_op] → B[target_align_op]
```

---

### 6.1 Complete Alignment Table (Illustrative)

| Source \ Target | `<` | `|` | `>` |
|-----------------|-----|-----|-----|
| `<` | L→L | L→C | L→R |
| `|` | C→L | C→C | C→R |
| `>` | R→L | R→C | R→R |

This table is illustrative only and derives directly from the Cartesian product definition.

---

## 7. Offset System

### 7.1 Syntax

```ebnf
OFFSET :=
  SIGN RATIONAL_NUMBER SPAN_ID

SIGN := "+" | "-"

RATIONAL_NUMBER :=
    POSITIVE_INTEGER "/" POSITIVE_INTEGER
  | POSITIVE_INTEGER

POSITIVE_INTEGER :=
  [1-9][0-9]*
```

Offsets are written without separators:

```text
-3/5span2
+1spanA
```

---

### 7.2 Semantics

```text
pos(A.anchor_i) =
  pos(B.anchor_j) + δ
```

Where:

```text
δ = ± (rational × width(SPAN_ID))
```

Offset expressions are edge properties of alignment constraints.

Span widths are immutable renderer-defined geometric inputs.

---

## 8. Graph Construction

PÂTE compiles into:

```
Directed multigraph G
```

- nodes = (span, anchor)
- edges = affine constraints

Each alignment expression produces exactly one edge.

---

## 9. Evaluation Model (Renderer Responsibility)

The renderer performs deterministic evaluation:

### Step 1 — Parse
Construct spans and anchor nodes.

### Step 2 — Build Graph
Translate alignment expressions into edges.

### Step 3 — Resolve Positions
Compute anchor positions satisfying:

```
pos(source_anchor) = pos(target_anchor) + δ
```

### Step 4 — Validate
A layout is valid iff:
- all constraints are satisfied exactly
- no conflicting assignments exist

No approximation or relaxation is permitted.

---

## 10. Validity Rules (STRICT)

A PÂTE program is valid only if:

### 10.1 Functional constraint rule
Each anchor has at most one defining constraint.

### 10.2 Consistency rule
If multiple constraints exist on the same anchor, they MUST be identical.

### 10.3 No conflict resolution
PÂTE does not:
- resolve contradictions
- prioritize constraints
- approximate solutions
- perform optimization

Conflicts are ill-formed input.

---

## 11. Alignment Semantics (Operational Form)

| Expression | Expansion |
|------------|----------|
| A << B | A.leading → B.leading |
| A >> B | A.trailing → B.trailing |
| A <> B | A.leading → B.trailing |
| A >< B | A.trailing → B.leading |
| A || B | A.center → B.center |
| A <| B | A.leading → B.center |
| A |> B | A.center → B.trailing |
| A |< B | A.center → B.leading |

## 12. Metric Constraint Layer (Post-Graph Typography Normalization)

This section defines an optional **metric shaping subsystem** that operates *after* PÂTE graph resolution. It does not participate in anchor graph construction, constraint propagation, or any SCC evaluation.

It is strictly a **render-time typographic adjustment layer**.

---

### 12.1 Purpose

The metric layer allows controlled adjustment of **internal span width** via interword spacing modulation so that a span’s rendered width matches a rational multiple of another span’s width.

This enables:

- proportional typographic alignment
- visual rhythm matching across spans
- controlled expansion/contraction of text density

without modifying spatial anchor positions.

---

### 12.2 Syntax

```
METRIC_CONSTRAINT :=
  SPAN_ID "~" RATIONAL_NUMBER SPAN_ID
```

Where:

- `SPAN_ID` = target span whose metric is being adjusted
- `~` = metric scaling operator (non-graph operator)
- `RATIONAL_NUMBER` = positive rational value
- `SPAN_ID` (reference) = span whose width defines the scale

Example:

```
A ~ 3/2 B
```

Meaning:

> render span A such that its width equals (3/2) × width(B)

---

### 12.3 Semantic Model

Each metric constraint defines:

```
width(A) = k × width(B)
```

Where:
- `k ∈ ℚ⁺`
- `width(B)` is computed after full PÂTE graph resolution

---

### 12.4 Execution Order

Metric constraints are evaluated **after** all PÂTE spatial constraints:

1. Parse CNML + PÂTE
2. Construct PÂTE constraint graph
3. Resolve anchor positions (graph evaluation)
4. Compute baseline span widths
5. Apply metric constraints
6. Perform final rendering pass

---

### 12.5 Non-Interaction Principle

Metric constraints MUST NOT:

- modify anchor positions
- introduce new graph edges
- participate in SCC evaluation
- affect alignment resolution
- recursively influence other metric constraints

They are strictly **post-hoc scalar adjustments**.

---

### 12.6 Dependency Rule

A metric constraint:

```
A ~ k B
```

is valid only if:

- `B` is already resolved in the PÂTE graph phase
- `B` does not depend on metric scaling of `A`

Cyclic metric dependencies are ill-formed.

---

### 12.7 Implementation Interpretation

Renderers MAY satisfy metric constraints using:

- interword spacing expansion/contraction
- glyph tracking adjustment
- font feature variation (if available)
- justification redistribution within span bounds

Renderers MUST NOT:

- alter anchor geometry
- re-run PÂTE graph resolution
- reinterpret alignment constraints

---

### 12.8 Determinism Requirement

Metric constraint evaluation MUST be deterministic given:

- resolved PÂTE layout
- fixed font metrics
- fixed rendering context

No iterative solving or convergence model is permitted.

---

### 12.9 Design Boundary

This layer exists explicitly to separate:

- **PÂTE** → geometric structure (discrete graph)
- **Metric layer** → typographic density (continuous adjustment)

These systems are intentionally non-recursive.

---

## 13. Canonical IR

All PÂTE programs reduce to:

```
Node = (Span, Anchor)
Edge = (Node → Node, δ)
Graph = Directed Weighted Multigraph
```

This is the only semantic substrate of PÂTE.

---

## 14. System Positioning (Final)

- CNML → structural + narrative representation layer
- PÂTE → spatial constraint compilation layer
- Renderer → graph evaluation + layout engine

> There is no Poem DSL runtime or intermediate execution layer.

---

## 15. Final Interpretation

PÂTE is:

> a Cartesian-product-based anchor alignment language that compiles into a deterministic affine graph, evaluated directly by the renderer to produce spatial layout.

Meaning is entirely encoded in graph structure, not in execution semantics.
